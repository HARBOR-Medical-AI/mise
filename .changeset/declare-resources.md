Tab completes the resource commands' subcommands, options and feeds; a bad option is refused before anything runs.

The `Resources` commands are each declared in one record: `feed`, `files`, `links`, `dirs`, `store`, `compute`, `tag`, `group`, `pluginmeta`, `plugininstance`, `workflow`, `parametersofplugin` and `whatif`, with their second names (`feeds`, `computes`, `tags`, `groups`, `pluginmetas`, `meta`, `metas`, `plugininstances`, `instance`, `instances`, `job`, `jobs`, `workflows`). Their lines are parsed against the grammar with GNU `getopt_long` rules, and `help` shows what each really takes: subcommands the help listed and no code ran (`tag create`, `group create`, `workflow rename`, `feeds delete`, `pluginmeta plugins`) are gone from it. A listing command run bare takes the same listing options as its `list` (`jobs --all`, `feeds --all --user radstar`), and `feed` alone lists the feeds where it printed its usage; a grammar's bare line may carry options of its own, parsed, completed, and shown in the usage, help and schema. `feed create <path>…` creates a feed from the folders named, as `--dirs` does; `compute search <term>` filters by name or field:value, where before it listed every resource; `store install` takes `--compute` and `--store` only (no `--adminUser`, `--adminPassword` or `--publicRepo`: `sudo store install` is the way). `files`, `links`, `dirs`, `store` and `parametersofplugin` with no subcommand, or a search with no term, are refused with exit status 1 where before they printed their usage and succeeded. The ChRIS backend registers two argument kinds: `feed`, which completes the feeds the session's index holds, and `compute`, which completes the compute resources CUBE lists. `whatif` declares an expression grammar, since its words are a whole line of another command, and completes that line as the line itself would. `plugin` and `pipeline` read files (descriptors, a parameter file), and stay undeclared until the change that rewrites file reads lands.
