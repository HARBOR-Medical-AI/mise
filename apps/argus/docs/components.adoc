== Abstract

AEGIS (`aegis.adoc`) is the doctrine: what the surface is, and the laws it keeps. This document is the reference beneath it: the components a pane is built from, one section each, in the shape a component library owes its users. A section says what the component is for, what it looks like (its anatomy, drawn and labelled in `images/`, and once the component is on stage, photographed in each of its states by the smoke driver), what a pane declares to get one, the states it can be in, the laws it carries so a pane does not have to, the stylesheet hooks it wears, and what not to do with it.

A component lands with its section, in the same pull request, and the section is amended by every slice that changes the component. A section, like the component it describes, speaks the component's own vocabulary and names no theme: colour reaches a component as tokens, and the doctrine above this reference is where the theme is named. The lint gate holds this table to the tree: every section names its source, and the sources that are components must have a section.

== Reading a section

Each section follows one order. *Purpose* says what the component answers. *Anatomy* opens with the drawing, then names its parts, outermost first, with the class each part wears; a component on stage adds a picture per state. *Declaration* is the TypeScript a pane writes, complete, with every optional field explained. *States* are the classes a part can wear and what puts them there. *Laws* names the AEGIS laws the component expresses as consequences of its declaration. *Stylesheet* lists the custom properties and selectors a theme may touch and the ones it may not. *Do not* lists the ways the component has been misused, or would be.

== Listing

Source: `apps/argus/src/features/roster/listing.ts` (the façade), over `order.ts` (sort and filter), `host.ts` (frame and field) and `row.ts` (traits, actions, progress, expansion).

Status: on stage in every tabular pane — the browser (2026-09-07, S2), the runs roster (2026-09-08, S3), PACS with its three levels (2026-09-08, S4), and the DICOM tags pane with four (module groups, tags, sequence items, their tags; 2026-09-09). No pane composes a listing by hand; the lint fails one that tries. The browser was the first pane converted, and its conversion is what turned this section from a declaration into a description of a thing that runs.

=== Purpose

A listing shows rows of one kind under column caps that sort them, behind a filter strip that narrows them, with a frame that stays while the rows scroll. It is the one tabular pattern of the surface: a directory's entries, a session's feeds, a query's studies and their series are all listings. A pane declares one and calls `rows_set`; it never assembles caps, strip, track or selection itself.

|===
| Part | Class | Holds

| grid host | (the mount, or `gridHost`) | The custom property `--roster-cols`, written by the façade from the traits. Every part below reads it.
| frame | `.roster-order` | The caps row and the filter strip, seated at the head of the mount, never re-inserted.
| caps | `.roster-caps` › `.roster-cap` | One cap per capped trait, blank cells over uncapped traits; the active cap wears `.roster-active` and its direction glyph. Every cap but the last carries a `.roster-grip` on its right edge: a drag moves that boundary with the pointer, and the two columns beside it trade the width within their floors (where one is the expanse, it gives or takes; `boundary_drag` in `features/roster/columns.ts`), a double-press gives both back; the row wears `.roster-sizing` during a drag. The grip is the gutter between two caps, centred on the boundary and wide enough for a finger: a touch drags it too.
| columns block | `<prefix>-columns` on the mode frame | Reads `COLUMNS · n HIDDEN` while columns stand aside for want of room (their names on hover; a trait's `shed` rank says which give way first, and a wider pane brings them back). Reads `COLUMNS` at rest and `COLUMNS · SIZED` once any column is hand-sized; a press gives every column back. What was sized is remembered per device (`localStorage`, `argus.columns.<prefix>.<trait>`), so a tablet and a desk keep their own.
| strip | `.roster-filter` › `.roster-filter-input` | The filter, hidden until FILTER or the language opens it.
| clear-new block | `runs-clearnew` on the RUNS roster's mode frame | Shown while any feed is NEW since the last visit (shared with this identity, not yet opened); a press takes every mark off (`proc seen all`). The NEW feeds lead the roster in a block of their own under `NEW SINCE YOUR LAST VISIT · n`, each marked `NEW · FROM <owner>`; opening one takes its mark off.
| jump block | `runs-jump` on the RUNS roster's mode frame | JUMP TO: the pane asks for a feed or node by number or name, runs `proc jump --where`, enters the feed and selects the node once its graph is drawn; a number that is both a feed and a node is asked again as a choice; nothing visible is said on the bar.
| refresh block | `<prefix>-refresh` on the mode frame | A press runs the pane's `refresh` declaration: the pane asks its source again, bypassing what the session remembers. The RUNS roster's runs `proc feeds --fresh` (the whole feed index walked now, off the lane) and the bar reads `CHECKING CUBE FOR THE FEED LIST…` until it lands, then `FEED LIST CURRENT · n NEW` or `· NOTHING NEW` for five seconds; the same line shows when the session walks on its own (login, a moved total), so a feed shared to this identity is seen arriving, never wondered about.
| field | `.listing-field` | The scrolling region beneath the frame, opened fresh on every render. A JPEG or PNG opened here is `.files-image`: it fills the field and keeps its shape (`object-fit: contain`), letterboxed on the field's black. Every scrolling field on the surface wears the `▼ N MORE` chip through `more_wire`; `MORE_FIELDS` in `features/roster/more.ts` is the list the lint holds the stylesheet to.
| block | `.listing-block` | One group of rows: a header when given, its own caps when `caps: 'each'`, then its rows or its painted form.
| rows | `.listing-rows` | The rows of one block.
| row | `.listing-row` | A grid on `--roster-cols`: one cell per trait, then the action track when actions are declared.
| control | `.listing-control` on the declared `control` trait's cell | A capsule that says what a press does — OPEN / CLOSE on a folding row, where the fold's state is the news; in the browser the kind's own glyph (the console's folder, file, cog) capped when the row opens, bare when it opens nothing, `..` wearing the folder like any directory; activates the row on a press and does nothing else.
| cell | (the trait's `className`) | What one trait says about one row.
| action track | `.listing-actions` | On a listing whose verbs stay on the row: reserved on every row at one declared height; holds the verbs of the indicated row, or every row's under `always`. Absent on a listing that declares a row zone.
| verb | `.listing-action` | One capsule, one command.
| readout | `.listing-readout` | Words beside the verbs — beneath them, in the row zone — about the indicated row only; or marks a pane builds (the runs roster's holders, `.holder-marks`, each with the × that withdraws its grant).
| level | `.listing-level` | A child listing beneath an open row: its own `--roster-cols`, its own caps, its rows.
| more chip | `.more-chip` on a `.holds-more` field, minted by `more_wire` (features/roster/more.ts) | At the foot of any field that scrolls — the listing field, the frame, a file's text, the band's rows — in place of a scrollbar: `▼ N MORE` while content lies below the view (N in the field's unit: rows, blocks, lines), `▲ TOP` at the foot; a press moves one page; hidden when everything is in view. The wheel and the finger still scroll. The band's wears a verb instead (`+N MORE · ON STAGE`). A field `more_wire` wired answers its edges too: meeting the top or the foot (a scroll come to rest there, or a wheel or finger pushing past it) sets `data-edge`, and the stylesheet flares that edge in `--edge-flare` and bounces the field's contents a few pixels; nothing moves under reduced motion.
| row zone | `.listing-zone` (also `<prefix>-row-zone`), minted by the façade into the mode frame beside the mount, above its fill | On the mode frame beneath the blocks that act on the field: the indicated row's verbs one under another with its readout beneath, or the selection's verbs while SELECT is on. Empty and hidden when nothing is indicated or selected; verbs arriving open the frame. A frame taller than its field scrolls, without a scrollbar: a chip at its foot (`.frame-more`) reads `▼ N MORE` while blocks lie below the view and `▲ TOP` at the foot, the press shows them; `modeFrames_watch` wires every frame as it is minted. The indicated row's bleed under the spine is clipped by the field (`overflow-x: hidden`), never scrolled.
| row cursor | `.listing-cursor` on a `.listing-row` (app/keys.ts) | The keys' place in a listing: ↑↓, Home and End move it over the focused pane's rows (a ring in `--butter`, over an indicated row and a plain one alike); Enter presses the row, and Enter on an indicated row goes, through its control cell where it declares one; → or Ctrl-B r takes the keys into the mode frame. Law a-frame-is-reachable-by-keys.

=== Declaration

[source,ts]
----
const listing = new Listing<FsListingEntry>({
  mount: panel,                       // the listing region
  traits: FILE_TRAITS,                // columns, each with a width; an uncapped one leads
  key: (entry) => entry.path,         // one string per row
  address: (entry) => entry.path,     // what the KERNEL calls it: the pill LIGHTS for a row @N reaches
  chrome: { root: pane, prefix: 'files' },
  rowZone: pane.querySelector('.files-row-zone'),   // the verbs ride the frame: no track
  actions: { of: (entry) => verbs_for(entry) },      // a row offered none keeps one click
  activate: (entry) => open(entry),
  activatable: (entry) => entry.type !== 'unknown',
  indicated: (entry) => regard_write(entry.path),
  row: { className: (entry) => `files-type-${entry.type}`, decorate: (el, entry) => { el.title = entry.path; } },
  selection: { verbs: (rows) => selectionVerbs_for(rows) },
  state: (parts) => ['CWD', listingState_compose(parts)].filter(Boolean).join(' · '),
  defaultSort: { key: 'name', dir: 'asc' },
});
listing.rows_set([{ key: '/x', lead: [UP], rows: entries }], { field: '/x' });
----

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Field | Meaning

| `mount` | The listing region. The frame seats at its head; the field opens beneath it.
| `traits` | The columns, in cap order. Each is a `ListingTrait`: key, label, `className`, `cell`, optional `compare`, and its `width` (a CSS grid track, required by the façade). `capped: false` makes a leading ornament: a track and a cell, no cap, no sort. Uncapped traits must lead; the façade refuses a declaration where one follows a capped trait.
| `key` | A row's identity as a string. Indication, selection, readouts and `row_element` are all keyed by it.
| `gridHost` | Where `--roster-cols` is written; defaults to the mount. A pane whose query form stands on the same grid outside the mount names an ancestor of both, and `template_get()` gives the form the template.
| `chrome` | The pane root and the block prefix. The façade finds `.pane-state`, `.<prefix>-filter` and `.<prefix>-select` under the root, binds them, and listens for `argus:roster` on the root. Absent, the listing has no chrome and writes no state line.
| `rowZone` | Rarely declared: a mount that stands in a framed field (a `.mode-frame` beside it) gets its zone MINTED there by the façade, above the frame's fill, wearing `.listing-zone` and `<prefix>-row-zone`. Name one only for a mount framed some other way. With a zone the listing's verbs ride the frame: no level mints a track (`actions.width` is not read), the mount wears `.listing-framed`, the indicated row's verbs are drawn in the zone and its readout beneath them, and a selection's verbs ride the same zone. Verbs arriving open the frame the zone stands on (`data-modes` on its `.workspace-pane`); the last of them leaving retracts it unless the operator opened it or has since pressed another block on it; the frame retracting stands the row down. A listing with no frame keeps the in-row track.
| `caps` | `root` (default) keeps one caps row in the frame; `each` mints a caps row at the head of every block instead, all reading one sort.
| `actions` | The row verbs. Without a `rowZone`, declaring this mints the action track: a track of `width` on every row, empty until the row is indicated, then holding `of(row)`; with one, no track, and `of(row)` is drawn in the zone. A row `of` offers nothing keeps its single click — a lead row too, so `..` enters on one click while `.` is offered the place's verbs and indicates. A level with a declared `control` keeps navigating apart from selecting: the control cell activates and selects nothing, the rest of the row selects and does not activate; without one, a folding row's click folds and indicates at once and a replacing row keeps click-indicates, double-click-goes. No double-click on a folding row. `always: true` draws every row's verbs at once and the click activates instead of indicating.
| `activate` | What activating a row does. With a `child`, activation also folds the row open or closed.
| `activatable` | Whether a row answers a click; absent means every row does. A row that does not wears no cursor and no listener.
| `indicated` | Told when a row is indicated, for the regard.
| `row` | The pane's own row-level concerns: extra classes (row state marks a row, not a column) and a decorator for title, dataset, whatever else.
| `selection` | Declaring this makes the listing able to SELECT: `verbs(rows)` gives the verbs for the current selection, drawn in the row zone, each one command over many operands. Requires a `rowZone`; the façade refuses a selection with nowhere to put its verbs.
| `state` | Composes the state line from typed parts (`filter`, `selecting`, `selected`, `shown`). Absent, `listingState_compose` writes `SELECT · FILTERED n/m · k SELECTED · s SHOWN`, each part only when it has something to say. Returning null leaves the span untouched, for a pane whose bar is saying something else.
| `defaultSort` | The initial sort.
| `terms` | Filter terms that are not columns: `key:text` keeps the rows whose value holds the text, and a bare word searches them too. The runs roster declares `tag`, so `tag:urgent` keeps the feeds wearing it, though the tags are marks beside the title rather than a column of their own.
| `control` | The key of the trait whose cell is the row's control: a press there activates the row (folds it on a level with a child; enters or opens it on one that replaces the listing) and does nothing else, while a press elsewhere on the row indicates it without activating. The cell wears `.listing-control`. Draw it as a capsule that reads what it will do next (OPEN / CLOSE from the group's state, OPEN, UP), ≥24px, inset so a miss lands on nothing; the façade refuses a `control` naming no trait.
| `child` | The level beneath: `listingChild_declare(of, declaration)`, where `of` gives a row's children and `declaration` is a `ListingLevel` for them (traits, key, actions, activate, its own `child`). A row that heads a level is wrapped with it in a `.listing-group` (plus `row.groupClassName`), which wears `.listing-open` while the row is open; the level beneath carries `data-depth` (1, 2, …) so a stylesheet indents by depth. The child's caps are minted per open row on the child's own grid, the parent's filter is read down the levels, and a parent survives the filter when a child matches. Activating a row folds it; `open_set(depth, keys)` holds rows open programmatically and `open_clear()` closes every level; `template_get(depth)` gives a form the level's template.
| `empty` | What the field says when blocks were set but hold no rows (an answer with nothing in it), as against no blocks at all (no answer yet), which says nothing.
| `filteredEmpty` | What the field says, given the filter's text, when rows were set but the filter keeps every one off stage; a pane that can act on a miss says so here. The RUNS roster's asks the session for a feed the filter names by id or `feed_N` (`proc feeds N`): a feed shared to this identity since the roster was walked arrives through the ask, and one CUBE does not list is answered with why. Absent, the field is empty under `FILTERED 0/n`.
| `refresh` | What the frame's REFRESH block does when pressed: the pane asks its source again. Absent, a REFRESH block in the markup does nothing.
|===

The methods a pane calls afterwards: `rows_set(blocks, { field })`, `row_indicate(key | null)`, `indicated_get()`, `indication_refresh()`, `readout_show(key, text | element)`, `row_element(key)`, `template_get()`, `painter_set(paint | null)`, `filter_toggle(open?)`, `select_toggle(on?)`, `select_isOn()`, `selection_get()`, `state_refresh()`.

A block is `{ key, header?, lead?, rows }`. A flat listing passes one; a browser listing several directories passes one per directory with a header each. `lead` rows are drawn before the ordered rows and stand outside the order: never sorted, filtered, counted or selected. Whether one has verbs is the roster's call as for any row: the browser's `..` has none and its UP capsule activates it. It wears its label on the capsule and no name beside it, and, being navigation and never the block, stays lit while the field recedes. A painter (`painter_set`) draws a block some other way, cards say, and receives the rows already ordered; a painted block has no grid, so its rows carry no track and no indication, and the painter owns what a click means.

As the browser uses it: one block per listed directory, each led by its lead rows — `~` (the way home, the house glyph in its capsule and the home path dimmed beside it, offered anywhere but home itself) above `..` (the way up, except at the root) — and headed by its path as a TRAIL (`.files-crumbs`): under home it starts at `~`, elsewhere at `/`, and every segment but the last (`.files-crumb-here`) is a press that goes there, lowered like any directory press (a following browser runs `cd`, a rooted one walks its own history). Home is the session's `/home/<user>`, told to each browser from the prompt (`home_set`); a catalogue, a picker over /bin, leads with neither. The row type carries the entry and the full path it sits at, since a listed name does not know its own directory. The card and preview projections are a painter; the cwd binding, the stale readout and the content views (a file, a JSON file as structure with RAW / PRETTY on its header, an image, a /bin entry — each the pane's field, scrolling inside it) are the browser's own, layered around the listing rather than inside it. A file click asks one rule what opens it (`imagery_is`): a NIfTI/MGZ volume or a DICOM slice is an image pane, wherever it was clicked — the browser, or a node's dive inside the graph. The browser declares its row zone and a control column, the kind's glyph in a capsule of the kind's console colour: a press enters a directory (`..` included), shows a file or graphs a catalogue entry, while its body puts the row's verbs in the frame — DOWNLOAD / MOVE / COPY / DELETE for a file, MOVE / COPY / DELETE for a directory, IMAGE and those for a DICOM series folder, SHARE FEED n inside a feed. What acts on the place on stage rides the frame (MKDIR, UPLOAD, REFRESH); the place is acted on as a row from its parent. A node overlay declares no verbs and no zone, so it keeps its single click. A node overlay (a dive into a node, in the graph or the universe) is that browser alone, covering the pane's frame so one frame stands — no strip of its own above it; at the node's root its way up reads EXIT NODE and leaves the node (`ceiling_set`), and EXIT NODE also stands first on its frame beside BACK; Esc keeps its first step back from a file to the listing. The same pane built with `{ catalogue: true }` is a CATALOGUE, and its header says so: `/bin` in catalogue traits (NAME / TYPE / VERSION), bound to an input by `binding_set` (a run strip above the listing, wrapping rather than overflowing a narrow pane: the binding readout, the editable line, and after a run a lit FEED capsule via `run_show`), its plugin rows offered RUN by the roster. In a bound catalogue the graph is the form: a plugin's one-node graph dives itself, the dive's parameter rows are VALUE cells (`.telemetry-input`, a check for a boolean) that write their flag into the strip's line through `features/files/runLine.ts` (`runLine_flagSet` / `runLine_flagGet`: the line is the only state, a hand edit stands), a pipeline node dives as `title · @id` editing `--<node>.<param>`, and RUN rides the diagram frame (`.diagram-run`) beside RANKED / 3D / PULSE. A catalogue leads with a RECENT block (`recent_set`, rows keyed under `/bin/recent`): the operator's lately-run executables from the kernel's `plugininstance.list` model, newest first. As a desktop it is the action `catalogue` (input, feed, node, the line verbatim) and a run's graph beside it the action `graph` (feed); restore returns the bound listing with its line, not the dive.

As PACS uses it: three levels, patient over study over series, one row zone for all three; the fold glyph is each folding row's own uncapped leading trait, drawn by the stylesheet from the group's open state; a level of one row is opened with `open_set`; a patient's and a study's fold cell (`control: 'fold'`) folds the row and nothing else, a study's body puts PULL STUDY in the frame, a series click puts GATHER / IMAGE / DIR / PULL there, and `indication_refresh` re-lights them on every stage change (GATHER from the cohort pane's answer, `gathered_is`); the workspace carries the study template from `template_get(1)` for the form, whose QUERY stands in the SERIES column; the summed progress bars and the per-series badges remain the pane's, painted into mounts its own cells register.

The progress track reads its own state, drawn by `progressCell_build`: an idle track when nothing is scheduled (a dim wash, never absent — nothing has happened yet is not the same as there is no such thing), a bright fill while running, a recessive fill when finished (`--pumpkin-pie`, the dark tone the ID/owner/created columns trade away to `--daybreak`, so a roster of finished feeds sits back and the running bars stand out), and, when the work errored, a fill in the error hue only as long as the work that succeeded (`successfulNodes / totalNodes`) — its length is how far the feed got before it died, not a full red bar that says nothing. The failed count reaches a roster row as `jobsErrored`; the fill falls back to the settled count without it. On the indicated row the track becomes a black well and the fill keeps a colour — the listing's light tint when done, the state's own while running or failed — never black: a black fill in a black-tinted track once made a series pulled to the last file read like one never fetched. A row's bar is the same drawing on every listing that shows the row: the cohort's series rows are the PACS answer's series rows, and take that listing's index hue (`--daybreak`), so a series looks the same wherever it is listed.

A pane's header is one line: its title ellipsises and carries the whole name as a tooltip, while the mode and state readouts beside it keep their width, whatever the pane is called.

The DASHBOARD (`features/launcher/panel.ts`, pane kind `launcher`, preset of its own, reached by DASHBOARD-01, the gutter's first block, or the word `dashboard`, and re-read on entry and on a fifteen-second beat while it is on stage) is not a listing: it is one block per domain, each a control. A block carries its name, figures (a count, a state, or a filter that opens the roster filtered), rows that are doors into recent work, and the verb it performs; the first block in the list takes the wide seat, so the domain with the most to say says it. Colour reaches a block as `--tile-hue` and its type is the frame's black-on-hue, so the contrast gate holds it like any other surface; nothing is faded, since a translucent chevron is a failed pairing waiting to happen. It is the empty state — `launcher_yield` hands the stage back to home the moment content opens — and `START HERE` on the block grid itself remembers whether a session begins there. The dashboard stands alone on the screen when it holds the stage: it is the zoomed pane (`data-zoom='launcher'`), so the header, the gutter and the console are away, and any press on a block, a figure or a row opens what it names with the gutter back, the header folded away and the console closed (the CONSOLE block opens the console); Esc gives the whole surface back, as do the top strip and anything else that takes the stage. Each block says what it is for in plain words (`features/launcher/about.ts`, operator-approved copy) between its bar and its rows; CONSOLE is a block of its own, wide, the typed door. The blocks are built by `app/dashboardTiles.ts` from the host's verbs.

As GATHER uses it (`features/gather/panel.ts`, pane kind `gather`, born below the PACS results by `gather_open` and joined to its group): two levels, the cohort row (`control: 'fold'`, opened by `open_set` since a level of one opens itself) over its series rows, keyed by series UID; the cohort row's body puts SAVE / EXPORT CSV / CREATE FEED / PROCESS / DISMISS in the row zone (PROCESS makes the cohort's feed if it has none, from the kernel's `feed.created` answer to `pull --new-feed`, then opens a catalogue bound to the feed's root; CREATE FEED is withdrawn once a feed exists; a FEED trait lights `FEED N` as a capsule opening the graph), a series row's REMOVE / IMAGE / PROCESS, both from the `gather.cohort` and `gather.series` rosters; the bar counts `n SERIES · m PATIENTS`; SAVE, EXPORT CSV and CREATE FEED ask the cohort's name once (`name_ask`) and lower to one visible command each; as a desktop the pane is the action `gather` (series, name, and the feed with its root when one was made). In the header band (the GATHER face) the same listing keeps the band's one height and scrolls inside it; while rows lie below the view a chip at its foot reads `+N MORE · ON STAGE` (`.gather-more`, N the rows below), and a press puts the whole cohort on stage as the frame's ON STAGE block does. The band's foot is a visible grip that drags its height (`#header-strip`), by mouse or finger.

The roster re-asks the session on its own beat while it is on stage (ten seconds while a listed feed is live, a minute when all are terminal), so a run started anywhere settles in place; whether it is on stage is the pane's own state, never read back from a style property.

As the roster uses it: one block, the constant field `roster`; a leading control column whose OPEN enters the feed, the row's body selecting it and putting SHARE / DELETE and the grant readout in the roster's own frame; PROGRESS is the expanse right after TITLE; the surface's row verbs are declared at construction (a roster given none keeps its single click); a feed's arrival pulse is a mark toggled on `row_element(key)` between renders; and the state composer yields the bar to the graph (returns null) whenever a graph rather than the roster is on stage. The pane's forwarders stringify the feed id at the boundary, since the façade keys rows by string.

=== States

[cols="1,1,3"]
|===
| State | Class | Put there by

| activating | `.listing-activating` on the pressed row; `state-wait` and `OPENING <name>` on the bar | The instant a row is activated, until the listing lands or the kernel refuses (then `state-refused` and the kernel's words on the bar). A pane whose answer is not the listing's own rows (a run's graph taking the DAG pane) calls `activation_settle()` when its answer lands, or the row comes back lit. The rest of the field dims as for an indicated row; the row's control capsule breathes. A repeat press on the same row is absorbed; a press on another row replaces it.
| indicated | `.listing-indicated` on the row | A click on a row of a listing with actions (outside its control cell, when it has one), or `row_indicate(key)`. Exactly one row at a time across every level; the previous row stands down. Belongs to the field: restored after a repaint of the same field (a fold, a sort, a re-listing), gone with a row that left, cleared by navigation. `indication_refresh()` redraws its verbs from their live predicates.
| selected | `.listing-selected` on the row | A click while SELECT is on, on a governed row. Kept across renders of the same field.
| selecting | `.listing-selecting` on the mount | `select_toggle`. The SELECT block reads `SELECT ON` and loses `rail-off`.
| framed | `.listing-framed` on the mount | A declared `rowZone`. The indicated row is drawn as a segment of the frame — the frame's block colour, black type, reaching the spine, its bars in black — and while one is, every other row recedes to a quarter (hover restores; not while selecting), so the block and the frame's verbs read as one pair. The zone wears `.listing-zone`.
| open | `.listing-open` on the row | Activation of a row that has a child level; its level follows it.
| activatable | `.listing-activatable` on the row | Any row `activatable` does not refuse.
| filtered | `FILTER ON` on the block, `FILTERED n/m` on the bar | The strip opening; the counts are taken across every block.
| numbered | `.numbered` on the row's `.listing-index` pill | The session's last answer holding this row's `address`. Row state that belongs to the pane (a refused read, an arrival, a status) goes through `row.className` at build or `row_element(key)` between renders; it is a mark on the row, never a column.

=== Laws

`a-row-is-indicated-before-it-is-acted-on`. Declared actions split click (indicate) from double-click (activate) on every row that is offered one; no actions, or a row offered none, means one click. Where the verbs stay on the row the track is present on every row at the height `.listing-actions` declares, so indicating changes what the track holds and never the geometry around it.

`a-row-s-verbs-live-in-the-frame`. A listing that declares a `rowZone` reserves no track: the indicated row's verbs are drawn in the frame's row zone, the row is drawn as a segment of the frame, verbs arriving open the frame, the last of them leaving retracts it unless the operator holds it, and the frame retracting stands the row down. A selection's verbs ride the same zone.

`a-selection-belongs-to-the-field`. `rows_set` names the field. The same name is the same field, so the selection survives a filter and a re-listing; a different name is navigation, so the selection clears. Leaving SELECT clears nothing. The selection's verbs ride the frame's row zone, not any row.

`caps-are-the-sort` and `roster-grid-single-source`. The caps are the frame, the track list is computed once from the traits and written as `--roster-cols`, and caps and rows read it alike. A pane never states a column count; the façade refuses a trait without a width at construction, so a miscount is a boot error and not a shifted row.

`listing-is-one-abstraction`. A pane declares its listing and never builds one. Reaching past the façade — to `RosterOrder`, `ListingHost`, the row or capsule builders, or a hand-typed `--roster-cols` — is the violation the lint names (S5); the PACS pane carries the one allowance, printed as debt, until S4.

`the-expanse-is-never-sized-by-hand`. A grip moves the boundary under it with the pointer; the two columns beside it trade, each within its floor, and a boundary never moves against the hand. The `1fr` expanse is never given a width: it gives or takes the difference while it can stretch, so bars keep one x across levels whatever was sized; pinned at its minimum, only that minimum moves. On a row wider than its pane the column simply grows and the row with it. The COLUMNS block reads SIZED while anything is, and gives all back.

=== Stylesheet

A track is a fixed length, or the listing's one expanse (`1fr`). Never a track sized to its content — `auto`, `minmax(0, …)`, `min-content`, `max-content`, `fit-content` — because a row is its own grid, and a content-sized track sizes per row: a short value narrows the track for that row alone and every column after it jogs. A listing that carries progress makes PROGRESS the expanse, right after its description column, with the trailing facts and the verbs after it. Levels beneath a level share the expanse's left edge: the leading tracks are the same widths on every level (or the child's description is widened to make up the difference), so the bars line up level over level. Every row and caps row of one listing shares one font size, since the tracks are in em. The façade refuses a content-sized track at construction (`listingTemplate_build` throws on `auto`, `minmax(0, …)` and the content keywords). The alignment across levels is declared, not computed: a level's leading tracks are given the same widths as its parent's (or its description widened by the difference), and the smoke suite measures the result.

The index pill is `.listing-index` inside the `.listing-index-cell` track: a capsule of the listing's own idiom. Lit, it carries the number the session reaches, in `--listing-index-hue` — a token each pane sets to its own FRAME hue, because the pill is chrome rather than content: a pill in the row's own colour sits beside the row's glyph and reads as one blob. Unlit, it carries the row's own count on a faint ground. A theme sets the hue per pane and may not resize the track, which the façade owns.

A theme may set, per pane: `--roster-gap` and `--roster-pad` on the grid host, the hues of `.listing-indicated` and `.listing-selected` and the framed row's block (`.listing-framed .listing-indicated`: the frame's block colour with black type), and the per-trait cell rules the trait's `className` names (alignment lives there, right-aligned numbers included). The row zone's capsules are `.listing-action` wearing the frame's geometry (`.mode-frame .listing-zone .listing-action`: full width, square, right-aligned, a label that wraps rather than cuts). A control IN a row — a fold cell, the browser's UP and OPEN — is a capsule in the frame's hue, dim at rest, lit on hover, inverted on the indicated block; never a bare glyph. The browser's kind glyphs and per-kind colours are the console's (`--console-<name>`, published by the terminal from its own palette; the Nerd Font icons of `colors.yml`), held equal by a unit test. A theme may not write `--roster-cols` on a converted pane, since the façade owns it; nor size `.listing-action`, whose height is declared once and read by the track that reserves room for it.

=== Do not

Do not size a track to its content, and do not put the expanse at the end of the row: a bar packed against the right edge after every fact is a readout nobody scans. Do not append a cell to a row outside the traits and the declared actions: a row one cell short or long shifts every cell of every row after it. Do not hold a row's element across a render; `rows_set` rebuilds the field, so ask `row_element(key)` again. Do not clear the selection from a pane; name the field and let the façade decide. Do not put a verb in a column; a verb answers to no cap and lives in the track or, on a framed listing, in the row zone. Do not find the row zone by prefix; declare it, since declaring it changes the grid. Do not use a painter to add a column; a painter replaces the grid, it does not extend it.

== Universe pane

Source: `apps/argus/src/features/universe/panel.ts` (the pane), over `features/universe/space.ts` (the graph built from landed feeds, the tip, the memory) and `scene/chrisSpace.ts` (the orrery reading ChRIS — `@fnndsc/orrery`'s scene, shared with the DAG pane).

Status: on stage since 2026-09-22 (slice 1 of the universe descent). Opens from the dashboard's UNIVERSE tile as a stage of its own (the `universe` preset, one primary pane raised whole, like RUNS and PACS); the wait's theater in the DAG pane (a universe drawn while the roster is refused) shares its graph and its tip but is not this pane.

=== Purpose

The universe pane shows the space of everything run on this CUBE: every feed the session's index holds, each drawn as its own small molecule — one sphere per plugin group, sized by the jobs it stands for and hued by the share of them that failed — pulled toward the feeds that share its pipeline shape, so the field reads as what this lab runs, how much of each, and where the red is. That is the FEEDS view, the default: the whole compute structure. The SHAPES view folds it: one molecule per pipeline shape, its spheres the shape's stages, each sized by the feeds folded under it and hued by how many of those feeds erred there — a thousand feeds of one bespoke pipeline as one molecule, `×1,000`; a click unfolds a shape into its members in place. In either view a click on a feed's sphere descends into the feed. It is its own pane kind: RUNS-02 stays a feed viewer, and the universe keeps its camera and its memory. It breathes with the index (landings while it warms, arrivals and status changes once it is whole) and remembers where its molecules settled, per identity.

=== Declaration

----
const panel = new UniversePanel(
  { canvas, title, state, empty, projectionPill, refreshPill },   // the template's parts
  { command_run: (line) => client.line_execute(line, { silent: true }).then(o => o.envelopes.forEach(panel.envelope_observe)) },
  localKeyStore(),                                                 // where the molecules' places are remembered

The cluster: in FEEDS a click on a halo (or `universe cluster <feed>`) lights the shape's feeds where they stand, dims the rest without moving anything, and flies the camera to frame them. In SHAPES the same click unfolds the folded molecule in place into its member feeds — each starting at the fold's centre and settling out, the rest frozen and dim, the settle a feed's descent uses. BACK, Esc or `universe back` climbs one level; a feed entered from a cluster climbs back to the cluster. `universe view feeds|shapes` switches the top; it refuses while inside.

The descent: a click on a sphere (or `universe enter <feed>`) flies the camera to that molecule, asks the host for the feed's graph (`feed_dag`, the kernel's `feed diagram`, which already collapses a fan into one node with a tally — the budget), unfolds the molecule into that graph in place with every other feed dimmed, and frames the nearest four fifths of the feed (a node must be wide enough to hover; the wheel reaches the outliers). While the ask runs the bar reads `UNIVERSE — ENTERING FEED n · title …` and the pill ASKING; an ask that yields nothing says so on the console, with the feed and the seconds waited. Inside, a click on a node shows its facts and verbs; the dimmed field behind the feed takes no pointer. Esc, BACK or `universe back` climbs out to the space as it has landed since; OPEN FEED puts the feed in a RUNS pane (`feed_open`). Landings and re-asks are taken while inside — and from the click until the feed is entered — and drawn on the climb; a repaint of the same space keeps a camera the operator has wheeled or dragged (law a-click-does-not-resettle-the-space). A wait on the field wears the wait readout (`WaitProgress`, `features/wait/progress.ts`, `.wait-progress`): bottom-left over the field, taking no pointer; `ASKING THE SESSION FOR THE SPACE` with a running bar until `proc universe` answers, then `SETTLING n SPHERES · p%` with a moving bar while a large settle runs in slices (law a-wait-over-two-seconds-shows-progress). A paint holds what already stands and settles only newcomers (`SettleMode` `new`); a scale or a view re-settles everything (`full`). The space turns about its focus — the point the camera looks at, set by a fit and by every flight: the idle spin and the orbit pivot on it, the wheel dollies toward it, a pan carries it, so an entered feed or a lit cluster turns in place in the middle of the frame. A pull on a sphere moves only its own molecule.

The pane asks one kernel command, `proc universe`, and reads one prompt field, `procWarmup.landed`; it never walks a feed itself. While the index warms each landing adds or reshapes a molecule (repaint at most once a second); once whole, a feed arriving through the roster delta or the feed count moving re-asks for the space (at most once in five seconds), so a new feed drifts in and a gone one leaves. Positions are written to the store under `argus.universe.<user>@<uri>` a few seconds after each settle and seed the next settle, so a reopened pane finds its molecules where they were.

=== States

* `.pane-state` `LANDING`: the index is still warming; the space grows with each landing.
* `.pane-state` `WHOLE`: the index is whole; the space stands, and moves only when the lab does.
* `.universe-canvas` hidden and `.universe-empty` shown: no space has arrived yet.
* `.universe-inside` on the pane, `.pane-state` `INSIDE`: a feed is entered; the title reads `INSIDE FEED <id> · <name> · <n> JOBS`; the OPEN FEED and BACK blocks stand on the frame.
* `.universe-cluster` on the pane, `.pane-state` `CLUSTER`: a shape is in view; the title reads `SHAPE <plugin> > <plugin> · <n> FEEDS`; BACK stands on the frame.

=== Laws

`a-control-lives-where-it-acts` (the pane's modes on its frame; a sphere's verbs will ride its overlay), `surface-never-shadows-the-session` (`proc universe` is the kernel's), `index-movement-never-holds-the-lane` (cache-resident asks only), `field-is-content`, `frame-retracts-to-a-strip`, `drawer-everywhere`.

=== Stylesheet

A theme may set the field's background and the tip's face. It may not put a control inside `.universe-canvas`, nor size the canvas other than 100% of its mount.

=== Do not

* Do not open the universe in the DAG pane, nor split it beside a browser: RUNS-02 is a feed viewer, and the universe is a stage, entered whole.
* Do not walk feeds from the surface to draw a molecule: the shape comes from the index, landed or whole.
* Do not repaint on every prompt: landings are batched to a second, re-asks to five.
* Do not unfold a feed past the kernel's own collapse: a fan stays one `×N` sphere inside; the facts carry the true count.
* Do not invent a verb inside a feed: a node offers what the DAG pane's node offers.

The calypso daemon restarted by its own operator, never from under them. Two controls ask: the standing RESTART pill beside LOG OUT, and the cure in the CALYPSO DAEMON OUT OF DATE notice. Both raise one question in the notice's spot. It names what a restart would cut off right now, and the door does the rest: it ends the daemon, boots a fresh one on the saved login, and sends the browser through the greeter. The operator stays logged in.

=== Anatomy

image::images/restart-question-anatomy.svg[Restart question anatomy,1180]

[cols="1,2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Part | Selector | What it is

| pill | `#restart-pill` | RESTART, beside LOG OUT in the header's controls; hidden without a door.
| cure | `.build-mismatch-restart` | "Restart the calypso daemon" in the out-of-date notice, as a control (behind a door only).
| question | `.stale-page.restart-question` | The notice's spot and clothes: the sentence, `.restart-cuts` (only what applies now), `.restart-answers` with `.restart-yes` (focused) and `.restart-no`.
| said | `.stale-page.restart-said` | What this page says while it restarts, or what another page says when the daemon closes ("…asked from another browser or surface. Following it…").
|===

=== Declaration

[source,ts]
----
// app/main.ts: the host hands it the unsaved edits and feeds it the wire
const restart = restart_wire({ unsaved: () => dirtyEditPaths });
const buildWatch = buildMatch_wire(statusBar, note, restart.ask); // the notice's cure asks too
restart.attach_take(attached.attach);              // this surface's own id
telemetry_receive: (index, extra) => restart.telemetry_take(extra); // lane + surfaces
closing_receive: (cause) => restart.closing_take(cause);
----

=== States

[cols="1,2,3",options="header"]
|===
| State | Where | Meaning

| hidden | the pill, the cure | No door: there is nothing to restart through, so the cure stays words.
| asking | the question | YES / NO; Enter and Esc; the stage sees neither key.
| restarting | `.restart-said` | YES pressed: the door was asked; the browser goes to the greeter.
| refused | `.restart-said` | The door could not restart it; its words are shown.
| followed | `.restart-said` on another page | The daemon closed with cause restart; this page follows to the greeter.
|===

=== Laws

* `a-daemon-is-restarted-by-its-own-operator`: asked first, by the operator's own press, behind a door. The door does it and keeps the login.
* `a-question-is-answered-where-the-hand-is`: the question stands where the notice stood.
* `a-surface-leaves-by-the-door-it-came-in`: RESTART stands beside LOG OUT only behind a door.

=== Stylesheet

`.restart-pill` (the door pills' shape, its own hue), `.restart-question` / `.restart-cuts` / `.restart-answers` / `.restart-yes` / `.restart-no` over the stale-page notice's frame. Colours are tokens.

=== Do not

* Do not restart without asking, and do not ask in the console: a door action is not a kernel line.
* Do not list what is not live: a generic warning teaches the operator to ignore the question.
* Do not show the controls without a door: a bare daemon has no supervisor to restart it.

== Feed view

Source: `apps/argus/src/features/feed/view.ts` (the modes, the device's memory of them, the frame over a pane's pills) and `apps/argus/src/scene/feedGraph.ts` (the one reading of a feed for the scene); mounted by the RUNS pane (`features/dag/panel.ts`) and by the universe on descent (`features/universe/panel.ts`).

Status: on stage since 2026-10-06 (#943, all five slices). Smoke scenario `feed-one-view`. Law `a-feed-has-one-view`.

=== Purpose

A feed opened from the RUNS roster and the same feed entered from the universe are one view: drawn by one builder, framed by one set of verbs, in the modes this device last chose, whichever door turned them. A feed opens as its tree, root on top (RANKED), unless the device chose a molecule.

=== Anatomy

image::images/feed-view-anatomy.svg[Feed view anatomy,1180]

[cols="1,2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Part | Selector | What it is

| verb pill | `[data-feed-verb]` on a pane's mode frame | One per mode: `strategy` (RANKED / MOLECULE), `projection` (3D / 2D), `draw` (SPHERES / STARS), `pulse` (PULSE ON / OFF), `metric` (TIME / SIZE), `census` (SHAPE / CENSUS), `gravity` (GRAVITY ON / OFF), `hue` (STATUS / COMPUTE). The pill reads the current state; `pulse` and `gravity` wear `.rail-off` while off.
| feed verbs | `.dag-note`, `.dag-tag`, `.dag-rename`, `.dag-marks` | The feed's note, tags and name (`features/dag/feedFrame.ts`), on both panes' frames.
| node facts | `.dag-facts`, `.universe-facts` › `.telemetry-row`, `.dag-subway`, `.feed-node-verbs` | One overlay for the picked node (`features/feed/facts.ts`): plugin, instance, status, count and faults of a `×N` group, wall time, size, data address, the line of stages, and ENTER NODE and PROCESS (refused on a group, which has no single instance to run on).
| universe swap | `.pane-universe.universe-inside` | Inside a feed the universe frame shows `.universe-feed` pills and hides the space-only ones (view, scale, arrangement, replay, refresh); its draw pill turns the feed view's draw while inside, and the space's own comes back on the way out.
|===

=== Declaration

[source,ts]
----
// A pane mounts the frame over its own pills and applies what turns.
this.feedView = new FeedViewFrame(modeFrame, {
  changed: (verb: FeedVerb, modes: FeedModes): void => this.feedMode_apply(verb, modes),
});
// A door opening a feed takes the device's memory again (the other door may have turned it).
const modes: FeedModes = this.feedView.recall();
const graph = feedGraph_build(model, { metric: modes.metric, hue: modes.hue, legend, draw: modes.draw, id_map });
----

=== States

Live in both doors: the pane holds the session's watch on the feed (`watch_set`), a fresh model of the same shape patches statuses in place (`feedShape_same`, `feedStatuses_patch`), any other redraws where it stands, and the readout says LIVE, SETTLED or STALE (the universe as `INSIDE · …`). Remembered per device in `argus.feedview`; defaults RANKED, 3D, SPHERES, PULSE OFF, TIME, SHAPE, GRAVITY OFF, STATUS. Every node is tubed whatever the draw and none is solid, so CENSUS shells every job of the feed; under SPHERES every node is a lit sphere, under STARS a point of light on a fine thread (tubes at a quarter width).

=== Laws

`a-feed-has-one-view`. The same feed is never drawn two ways by two doors: one builder, one frame, one remembered set of modes.

A readout for a smoke, beside the fit count: the scene writes `data-roots-top` (`yes` / `no`) on its canvas after each draw, from the lit nodes' heights (`orrery/scene/tree.ts`), so a feed opened as its tree, root on top, can be said through either door.

=== Stylesheet

`.pane-universe:not(.universe-inside) .universe-feed` and the space-only pills under `.universe-inside` are hidden by display; the pills themselves are the frame's `.strategy-pill`.

=== Do not

Do not give a pane its own copy of a feed mode, or read a feed's model into the scene anywhere but `feedGraph_build`.
